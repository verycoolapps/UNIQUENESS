# Domain Frameworks — Domain-Specific Analytical Lenses

> Tailored analytical frameworks for each major subject domain. Select the matching framework, then adapt it to the specific subjects.

---

## Domain A — Business & Commerce

**Core Question**: What makes this business genuinely harder to substitute than its alternatives?

### 10 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Business Model Architecture** | How does it make money? What is the unit economics story? Is the revenue mechanism itself distinctive? |
| **Customer Segment Precision** | Who exactly does it serve? Is the segment defined with unusual specificity? Does it serve a genuinely underserved group? |
| **Value Proposition Clarity** | What specific problem does it solve? Is the problem statement precise, or is it a generic "we help people do X better"? |
| **Competitive Moat** | What makes the advantage durable? Network effects, switching costs, proprietary data, regulatory license, brand capital, operational scale? |
| **Distribution Mechanism** | How does it reach customers? Is the distribution channel itself differentiated (e.g., community-led, word-of-mouth-only, embedded in existing workflows)? |
| **Operational Distinctiveness** | How does it execute? Are there operational decisions that competitors could observe but not easily replicate? |
| **Talent & Cultural DNA** | What type of people does it attract and retain? What does the team believe that shapes product and strategy? |
| **Strategic Philosophy** | What theory of winning guides decisions? Is it defensively or offensively positioned? Long-term or short-term oriented? |
| **Economic Structure** | What are the margin mechanics? Is cost structure distinctive? Does it have pricing power that competitors lack? |
| **Brand Relationship** | What emotional contract does it have with its market? Is the brand a reason to choose, or just a label? |

**Typical differentiators**: Business model innovation (not just features), proprietary data advantages, community moats, founder credibility in a niche, pricing model distinctiveness

**Common uniqueness traps**:
- Confusing "being good at execution" with structural differentiation — competitors can hire their way to execution parity
- Claiming brand differentiation when brand is just a logo — real brand differentiation shows up in pricing power
- Treating market share as evidence of uniqueness — dominance and distinctiveness are different things

## Domain B — Technology & AI

**Core Question**: What technical decisions or capabilities create lasting advantages that competitors cannot quickly replicate?

### 10 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Core Technical Architecture** | What fundamental design decisions shape the entire system? Are these decisions defensible or reversible? |
| **Data Advantage** | Does the product generate, accumulate, or access data that competitors cannot access easily? Does the data get better with use? |
| **Algorithm Distinctiveness** | Is there a novel algorithmic approach, or is it standard? Is the approach patented? Is it proprietary trained data? |
| **Developer & Integration Ecosystem** | Does it have an ecosystem that creates lock-in through integrations, extensions, or trained user habits? |
| **Performance Benchmarks** | Does it demonstrably outperform alternatives on metrics that actually matter to users? |
| **Privacy & Security Architecture** | Are privacy/security decisions architecturally distinctive, or feature-level? |
| **Latency & Reliability** | Does the infrastructure architecture produce user-perceptible performance differences? |
| **UX Philosophy** | Is the interaction design distinctive in a way that creates habit formation? |
| **Deployment Model** | Cloud, edge, hybrid, on-premise — does the deployment model create access to markets competitors cannot serve? |
| **IP & Patent Landscape** | What is owned, protected, and therefore off-limits to competitors? |

**Typical differentiators**: Proprietary training data, architectural decisions with compounding advantages (data flywheels), ecosystem lock-in, specific regulatory compliance built into core design, edge deployment capability

**Common uniqueness traps**:
- Features are not moats — they can be copied in months
- "Built on the same underlying model as everyone else" = no technical differentiation at the foundation
- Performance claims without methodology are marketing, not differentiation

## Domain C — Creative Industries

**Core Question**: What makes this work feel like it could only have come from this specific creator?

### 10 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Concept Originality** | Is the central idea genuinely new, or a skilled execution of an existing concept? Where did the concept originate? |
| **Style Signature** | Is there a recognizable visual/sonic/narrative style that would be identifiable without a label? |
| **Compositional Decisions** | What specific structural choices (layout, rhythm, sequence, negative space) are distinctive? |
| **Cultural Embedding** | What cultural context or tradition does this work engage with? Is that engagement conventional or surprising? |
| **Emotional Mechanics** | What specific emotional responses does the work engineer? How does it achieve them differently than alternatives? |
| **Craft Decisions** | What material, technical, or medium-specific choices contribute to its identity? |
| **Thematic Consistency** | Across a body of work: what themes recur? Do these themes create a recognizable creative identity? |
| **Influence & Reference** | What is this work in conversation with? Does it extend, challenge, or ignore its creative lineage? |
| **Execution-Concept Alignment** | Does the execution reinforce the concept, or does it undermine it? Alignment here is a differentiator. |
| **Audience Contract** | What expectations does this work create and then fulfill (or deliberately subvert)? |

**Typical differentiators**: A specific unresolved tension the creator consistently explores, a technique that creates a physically distinctive output (Cindy Sherman's self-portraiture concept), cultural hybridity that couldn't emerge from a creator without that specific background

**Common uniqueness traps**:
- Technical skill ≠ originality. Flawless execution of a conventional concept is "exceptional" not "original"
- Style ≠ identity. Many creators have a consistent style without having a distinctive creative identity
- Awards ≠ uniqueness. Recognition reflects peer valuation in the period when it was awarded

## Domain D — Human & Talent

**Core Question**: What combination of skills, experiences, and perspectives makes this person the specific right choice for a specific type of work?

### 10 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Skill Rarity** | Is any individual skill genuinely rare, or is the combination of skills what's rare? |
| **Cross-Domain Synthesis** | Does this person operate across domains in ways that create non-obvious connections? |
| **Demonstrated Outcomes** | What verifiable outcomes exist? Are these outcomes reproducible across contexts, or one-time? |
| **Knowledge Depth vs. Breadth** | Where is deep expertise vs. working knowledge? Is the T-shape of knowledge itself distinctive? |
| **Communication Style** | Is there a distinctive way this person explains, persuades, or connects? Can this be clearly described? |
| **Problem-Solving Signature** | Is there a recognizable approach to challenges? A particular framework or lens consistently applied? |
| **Network & Access** | Does access to specific communities, relationships, or information sources create unique leverage? |
| **Failure Experience** | What has this person navigated that others haven't? Difficult failure experience is often undervalued distinctiveness. |
| **Cultural Fluency** | Can this person operate authentically in multiple cultural contexts that rarely overlap? |
| **Learning Velocity** | Is there a track record of acquiring new skills faster than peers? This is a meta-skill that compounds. |

**Common uniqueness traps**:
- Generic skill claims ("strategic thinker," "collaborative," "results-driven") — these describe almost every professional
- Credentials ≠ capability — what can this person actually do, and what's the evidence?
- Adjacent expertise ≠ genuine cross-domain synthesis

## Domain E — Education & Knowledge

**Core Question**: What about this learning experience, framework, or body of knowledge cannot be approximated by alternatives?

### 8 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Pedagogical Philosophy** | What theory of learning drives the design? Is that philosophy distinctive in the domain? |
| **Knowledge Synthesis** | Does this framework integrate knowledge from sources that existing frameworks keep separate? |
| **Empirical Grounding** | Is this framework validated against real-world outcomes, or is it theoretical? |
| **Accessibility Architecture** | Who can learn this? Is the accessibility itself a differentiator (designed for different age groups, learning styles, cognitive differences)? |
| **Application Transfer** | How well do learners transfer knowledge to real-world situations? Is transfer rate measurable? |
| **Conceptual Compression** | Does this framework distill complex ideas into genuinely simpler, more usable forms? |
| **Community & Practice** | Is there a community of practitioners that creates learning effects unavailable through self-study? |
| **Adaptability** | Can the framework be applied across domains beyond its original context? |

## Domain F — Physical Products

**Core Question**: What about this object's materials, manufacturing, design, or interaction is genuinely difficult to replicate?

### 10 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Material Decisions** | What specific materials were chosen and why? Are sourcing decisions creating quality or ethical differentiation? |
| **Manufacturing Process** | How is it made? Are manufacturing decisions creating quality, consistency, or capabilities that alternatives lack? |
| **Interaction Design** | How does the user physically interact with it? Are there tactile, visual, or behavioral interactions that create distinctive experiences? |
| **Durability Architecture** | How is it designed to age? Does it wear in a distinctive way (patina, upgrade path, repair philosophy)? |
| **Performance Specifications** | What does it do better than alternatives in ways that matter to users? Are specs verified independently? |
| **Safety & Compliance** | Does it exceed regulatory minimums in ways that create meaningful differentiation? |
| **Environmental Impact** | What is the lifecycle sustainability story? Is it verifiably different from alternatives? |
| **Serviceability** | Can it be repaired, upgraded, or maintained in ways alternatives cannot? |
| **Packaging & Unboxing** | Is the packaging itself part of the product experience in a distinctive way? |
| **Country/Region of Origin** | Does geographic origin create authentic differentiation (not just labeling, but craft traditions, materials, or quality standards)? |

## Domain G — Experiences & Lifestyle

**Core Question**: What specific combination of sensory, emotional, social, and narrative elements creates an experience that cannot be replicated elsewhere?

### 8 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Experience Architecture** | How is the experience designed from first contact to exit? What is the emotional journey intended? |
| **Sensory Distinctiveness** | What do visitors see, hear, smell, taste, and touch that is genuinely unusual? |
| **Social Dynamics** | What relationships does the experience facilitate or change? |
| **Staff Interaction Design** | What is the human element of the experience designed to create? |
| **Ritual & Ceremony** | Are there specific rituals or ceremonies embedded in the experience? |
| **Personalization Depth** | To what degree is the experience adapted to the individual vs. standardized? |
| **Memory Engineering** | What specific elements are designed to be remembered? |
| **Community Creation** | Does the experience create ongoing relationships among participants? |

## Domain H — Strategy & Ideas

**Core Question**: What makes this strategy or idea genuinely more insightful, more applicable, or more powerful than alternatives for solving the same challenge?

### 8 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Problem Framing Originality** | Does this idea reframe the problem itself, or only propose a new solution to an existing framing? |
| **Mechanism Clarity** | Is the causal mechanism by which this idea works clearly articulated and testable? |
| **Generalizability** | Across how many contexts can this strategy apply without losing its core insight? |
| **Counterintuitive Elements** | Does this idea contain something that challenges conventional wisdom in a defensible way? |
| **Empirical Support** | What evidence exists that this approach works? |
| **Implementation Specificity** | Does this strategy include specific enough guidance to actually implement, or is it inspirational but vague? |
| **Failure Mode Awareness** | Does the strategy identify when it would fail, and why? |
| **Source Diversity** | Was this idea synthesized from an unusually diverse range of sources or disciplines? |

## Domain I — Science & Nature

**Core Question**: What specific characteristic of this organism, phenomenon, or discovery is rare, poorly understood, or scientifically significant?

### 8 Key Analytical Lenses

| Lens | Questions to Ask |
|------|-----------------|
| **Taxonomic Rarity** | How many known instances exist in the defined biological or physical system? |
| **Mechanism Novelty** | Is the mechanism by which this phenomenon occurs understood, and is that mechanism itself unusual? |
| **Ecological or Systems Role** | What unique role does this play in its broader system? Could the system function without it? |
| **Evolutionary or Historical Distinctiveness** | What historical path produced this characteristic? Is that path rare? |
| **Research Gap** | Is this a poorly studied phenomenon that may be more significant than currently recognized? |
| **Applied Potential** | What applications — technological, medical, environmental — does this uniqueness enable? |
| **Observable Detection** | How difficult is this characteristic to detect or measure? Difficulty of observation can itself be significant. |
| **Stability** | Is this characteristic stable across conditions, or context-dependent? |

## Cross-Domain Comparison Methodology

When comparing subjects across different domains (e.g., comparing a business against an individual, or a physical product against a digital service), apply this protocol:

1. **Identify the shared purpose dimension**: What are both subjects trying to achieve for a user/audience?
2. **Find 3–5 dimensions where both subjects can be meaningfully evaluated**: Focus on outcome-level dimensions, not feature-level ones
3. **Acknowledge the frame explicitly**: State that this is a cross-domain comparison and define what insight it is designed to generate
4. **Apply domain-adjusted weights separately for each subject**: A score of 7 for Originality means different things in a business context vs. a creative context
5. **Draw cross-domain insights last**: After evaluating each subject on its own terms, identify what one domain reveals about the other
