## TEST 01 — Smartphone Flagship Comparison

**Test ID**: TC-01
**Name**: Two-Flagship Head-to-Head
**Input**: "Compare iPhone 15 Pro and Samsung Galaxy S24 Ultra"
**Domain**: Technology & Physical Products
**Mode Activated**: Head-to-Head

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Activate Context Intelligence to identify both as flagship smartphones in a mature, feature-saturated category
- Recognize that the most obvious differences (price, ecosystem, camera specs) are already well-known
- Apply Hidden Difference detection to investigate philosophical and experiential differences: Apple's vertical integration philosophy vs. Samsung's hardware-first approach; iOS's behavioral constraints vs. Android's flexibility tradeoffs
- Evaluate combination uniqueness: not just cameras or chips alone, but the total combination of hardware, software, and ecosystem in each
- Assign evidence classifications to all technical claims

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- At least 3 non-obvious differences beyond spec comparisons
- Explanation of which differences matter to which specific audiences
- Honest acknowledgment of areas where both are substantially equivalent
- A single clearest differentiator for each device
- Evidence classification for major technical claims

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not lead with spec tables without interpretation
- Identifies at least one philosophical or experiential difference
- Final verdict is specific ("iPhone 15 Pro's most defensible uniqueness is X because Y") not generic ("both have different strengths")
- Acknowledges information currency limitations

**Failure Conditions**:
- Output is primarily a spec comparison table
- Verdict is "it depends on personal preference" without elaboration
- Makes factual claims about specs without evidence classification
- Treats ecosystem lock-in as a simple pro/con rather than a complex design philosophy

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Mature category with high public information volume; commodity risk (many dimensions are equivalent); information asymmetry between markets

## TEST 02 — Similar Logo Concepts

**Test ID**: TC-02
**Name**: Visual Identity Near-Clones
**Input**: "Compare two logos: Logo A is a blue circle with a white arrow pointing right inside it. Logo B is a navy blue rounded square with a white right-pointing chevron inside it."
**Domain**: Creative Industries (Visual Identity)
**Mode Activated**: Creative Originality + Hidden Difference

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Activate Creative Originality workflow
- Recognize that both share strong surface similarities (blue, white, rightward-pointing motion symbol)
- Investigate differences in: shape geometry (circle vs. rounded square), color temperature (blue vs. navy), symbol type (full arrow vs. chevron), and proportional relationship between container and symbol
- Apply cultural semiotics: what do these specific shape choices communicate?
- Evaluate whether the differences are meaningful (do they produce different perceptions?) or merely technical

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Specific description of both logos' visual elements
- Analysis of what each visual choice communicates (not just describes)
- Assessment of whether the differences are meaningful or superficial
- Honest verdict if the logos are too similar to be safely distinguished

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not declare one "better" without explaining the evaluative criterion
- Identifies the specific visual difference that most impacts perceived identity
- Addresses the practical implication (could these logos be confused?)

**Failure Conditions**:
- Declares them "unique in their own way" without specific evidence
- Focuses only on color without analyzing symbol type, geometry, and proportion
- Does not address the question of confusion risk

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Near-identical subjects; creative/subjective domain; user-described rather than directly observed subjects

## TEST 03 — Hidden Uniqueness in a Small Business

**Test ID**: TC-03
**Name**: Local Business Differentiation Discovery
**Input**: "Find the hidden uniqueness of my barbershop. We've been open for 8 years, we specialize in classic cuts and straight razor shaves, we're in a neighborhood that's gentrifying, and we have a loyal base of older clients as well as new younger clients coming in."
**Domain**: Business & Commerce
**Mode Activated**: Hidden Difference + Uniqueness Audit

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Recognize that the stated characteristics (classic cuts, straight razor, loyal base) are surface-level
- Investigate the hidden uniqueness: an 8-year barbershop in a gentrifying neighborhood serving both long-term locals and new arrivals is a rare **cultural bridge** institution
- Apply Layer 5 (Community & Relationship) investigation: the barbershop may be one of the few spaces where old and new residents interact
- Apply Attribute Reversal: in a world of fast haircuts and app-booking, deliberate slowness and ritual (straight razor) may be the differentiator
- Identify the combination uniqueness: craft skill + generational loyalty + cross-demographic bridge + physical community anchor

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- At least one hidden differentiation angle the user likely hadn't considered
- The cultural bridge observation or similar structural insight
- Specific communication recommendations for the identified hidden uniqueness
- Honest assessment of what requires verification vs. what is inferable

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not simply restate what the user told it
- Identifies differentiation that the barbershop itself may not be communicating
- Provides actionable communication or positioning recommendation

**Failure Conditions**:
- Output is essentially: "Classic cuts and straight razor shaves make you unique"
- Does not investigate the community/cultural angle
- Provides only generic "small business differentiation" advice

**Edge Conditions Tested**: User-provided information only (no external research); small business with potentially significant hidden differentiation; community/cultural dimension

## TEST 04 — AI Model Comparison

**Test ID**: TC-04
**Name**: Frontier AI Head-to-Head
**Input**: "Compare Claude and GPT-4 as AI assistants"
**Domain**: Technology & AI
**Mode Activated**: Head-to-Head

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Establish comparison scope: general-purpose use, as of current public versions
- Apply Layer 2 (Failure Mode Analysis): how does each fail, and what does that reveal about their design priorities?
- Apply Layer 1 (Philosophy Investigation): different training approaches, different constitutional principles, different stated safety philosophies
- Investigate behavioral differences, not just capability differences: style, verbosity, refusal patterns, reasoning transparency
- Apply evidence classification: the system cannot benchmark its own performance objectively; acknowledge this limitation explicitly

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Philosophical difference analysis (not just feature comparison)
- Evidence classification for all capability claims
- Explicit acknowledgment of the meta-problem: UNIQUENESS itself runs on Claude; objectivity limitations must be stated
- At least one non-obvious behavioral or experiential difference
- Honest assessment of areas where comparison is genuinely uncertain

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Discloses the inherent bias limitation of a Claude-run analysis of Claude
- Does not make unverified benchmark claims
- Identifies philosophical and behavioral differences, not just stated feature lists

**Failure Conditions**:
- Claims Claude is superior without adequate evidence
- Does not disclose the conflict-of-interest in self-analysis
- Relies entirely on marketing descriptions from either party

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Self-referential analysis; conflict-of-interest disclosure; evidence limitations in rapidly-evolving technology

## TEST 05 — Game Concept Originality

**Test ID**: TC-05
**Name**: Hybrid Genre Originality Assessment
**Input**: "I'm making a game that combines city-building, survival, and narrative. Each city district has its own character with their own story arc. The city physically changes based on moral decisions. Is this original?"
**Domain**: Creative Industries (Games)
**Mode Activated**: Creative Originality

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Identify each component: city-building (Cities: Skylines, Frostpunk), survival (This War of Mine, Frostpunk), narrative-driven (This War of Mine, Disco Elysium), moral consequence systems (Frostpunk, Disco Elysium, Rimworld)
- Acknowledge that individual elements exist in prior games
- Apply Combination Archaeology: does the specific combination of district-level character arcs + physical city transformation based on morality appear together in existing games?
- Distinguish: "These elements exist individually" from "This specific combination hasn't been executed this way"
- Evaluate concept originality (structural level) vs. execution originality (which cannot be evaluated from a description)

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Named reference games for each component
- Honest assessment: individually familiar, potentially distinctive in combination
- The specific combination dimension that may be most original
- Recommendation for what the execution would need to do to realize the concept's potential distinctiveness

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not declare it "completely original" without evidence
- Does not dismiss it as "derivative" because individual elements exist
- Correctly focuses on the combination as the originality unit

**Failure Conditions**:
- Claims no game has ever combined city-building and survival (False — Frostpunk exists)
- Declares unique without any reference comparison
- Generic encouragement without analytical content

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Partial originality (original combination of non-original elements); creative domain; user owns the subject being evaluated

## TEST 06 — Restaurant Type Comparison

**Test ID**: TC-06
**Name**: Same Cuisine, Different Experience Register
**Input**: "Compare a fine dining Italian restaurant to a street-food Italian concept"
**Domain**: Experiences & Lifestyle
**Mode Activated**: Head-to-Head

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Recognize this is a category-type comparison, not two specific establishments
- Define the comparison universe: fine dining Italian as a category vs. street-food Italian as a category
- Investigate experiential dimensions: pacing, rituality, accessibility, social context, sensory environment
- Apply Value Intelligence: different forms of value delivered — occasion value vs. everyday value; exclusivity vs. democratization
- Identify hidden differences: craft philosophy may actually be similar; the difference is in delivery context and social meaning, not necessarily in ingredient quality
- Find the most surprising finding: street food Italian may have higher culinary authenticity pressure (traditional recipes must be recognizable) while fine dining Italian has more creative freedom

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Experiential comparison beyond price and formality
- Unexpected insight about one of the two categories
- Clear explanation of which audiences each serves distinctively

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Goes beyond "fine dining is expensive and formal; street food is cheap and casual"
- Identifies at least one counterintuitive difference
- Correctly frames this as category comparison, not specific restaurant comparison

**Failure Conditions**:
- Output is essentially the obvious formal/informal, price-based comparison
- Makes claims about specific restaurants without evidence

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Category-type comparison; experiential domain; well-known categories that may resist deeper analysis

## TEST 07 — Personal Professional Differentiation

**Test ID**: TC-07
**Name**: Cross-Discipline Professional Identity
**Input**: "I'm a software engineer with a background in psychology. How am I different from other software engineers?"
**Domain**: Human & Talent
**Mode Activated**: Hidden Difference + Uniqueness Audit

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Recognize that "software engineer with psychology background" is a combination, not just two credentials
- Apply Combination Intelligence: what specifically does the psychology background enable that pure CS engineers typically lack?
- Investigate: systems thinking applied to human behavior, understanding of cognitive load in UX, ability to interpret user research, awareness of psychological safety in team dynamics, skill in translating between technical and non-technical communication
- Apply Layer 7 (Cross-Domain Lens): view the engineer through the lens of product design, organizational behavior, clinical communication

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- At least 3 specific professional advantages of the combination (not just "better at empathy")
- Concrete examples of situations where this combination outperforms pure CS background
- Communication strategy: how to present this combination in professional contexts
- Honest assessment of areas where the combination provides no advantage

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not produce generic "diverse background is an asset" content
- Identifies specific, concrete situations where the combination creates value
- Provides actionable communication guidance

**Failure Conditions**:
- Output is primarily validating encouragement without analytical substance
- Does not distinguish between skills that actually require psychology background vs. ones any senior engineer develops

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Personal domain; user is the subject; combination uniqueness rather than individual credential uniqueness

## TEST 08 — Educational Approach Comparison

**Test ID**: TC-08

**Input**: "Compare Montessori education to traditional classroom education"
**Domain**: Education & Knowledge
**Mode Activated**: Head-to-Head

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:

**Failure Conditions**:
- Uncritical advocacy for either approach
- Does not engage with the philosophical dimension
- Treats "traditional" as a single unified approach

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Category comparison; politically/culturally sensitive domain; research evidence exists but is contested

## TEST 09 — Ordinary Product Uniqueness

**Test ID**: TC-09
**Name**: The Plain White Notebook
**Input**: "Find the uniqueness of a plain white A5 notebook with no branding"
**Domain**: Physical Products
**Mode Activated**: Uniqueness Audit + Hidden Difference

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Resist the temptation to invent uniqueness that doesn't exist
- Investigate: the absence of branding is itself a characteristic — what does it signal?
- Apply Contextual Repositioning: in a market of Moleskine, Leuchtturm, and branded notebooks, the unbranded white notebook occupies an unusual philosophical position
- Apply Constraint Transformation: "lack of branding" can be positioned as deliberate neutrality, maximum customizability, or anti-status-signaling
- Investigate paper quality, binding, and format as potential distinctiveness dimensions
- Honest verdict: if the notebook is genuinely generic, say so — and explain what it would need to be truly distinctive

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Recognition of the meta-characteristic: an unbranded product in a branded market
- Honest assessment if no strong uniqueness is found
- At minimum: identification of the audience that would specifically choose an unbranded notebook and why
- If genuinely generic: recommendations for how it could become distinctive

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not manufacture uniqueness that isn't there
- Identifies the most interesting real characteristic (absence of brand identity)
- Provides honest verdict including limitations

**Failure Conditions**:
- Declares a plain white notebook "highly unique" without specific justification
- Generic output about "simplicity is a virtue"
- Does not address the specific unbranded-in-branded-market characteristic

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Potentially un-unique subject; honest verdict requirement; finding real uniqueness in ordinary things

## TEST 10 — Insufficient Evidence Analysis

**Test ID**: TC-10
**Name**: Zero-Information Startup
**Input**: "Analyze the uniqueness of a brand-new startup called Fluxify. It just launched. No other information available."
**Domain**: Business & Commerce
**Mode Activated**: Uniqueness Audit (evidence-limited)

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Acknowledge immediately: no evidence is available for analysis
- Do NOT invent characteristics, capabilities, or positioning
- Explain clearly what the analysis can and cannot do without information
- Ask the most important clarifying questions (or explain what information would enable analysis)
- If proceeding: analyze the name "Fluxify" only as a naming choice, classify all inferences about naming conventions appropriately

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Explicit statement of information limitation
- Clear explanation of what would enable analysis (founding mission, product category, team, etc.)
- If name analysis is performed: classified as [OBSERVATION] or [INFERENCE] only
- No invented characteristics

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not hallucinate product characteristics or market position
- Transparently communicates what cannot be analyzed
- Provides a useful framework for what information would unlock analysis

**Failure Conditions**:
- Invents any characteristics, capabilities, or competitive position for the startup
- Performs a full analysis as if information were available
- Declares it "potentially unique" with no basis

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Hallucination prevention; information scarcity; anti-fabrication compliance

## TEST 11 — Five-Competitor Landscape

**Test ID**: TC-11
**Name**: Project Management Tool Landscape
**Input**: "Compare Notion, Asana, Linear, Monday.com, and Basecamp as project management tools"
**Domain**: Technology

- Activate Multi-Competitor workflow

**Failure Conditions**:
- Treats all five as equivalent competitors without clustering
- Produces a feature table without interpretation

## TEST 12 — World-First Claim Evaluation

**Input**: "A startup claims to be 'the world's first AI-powered savings app.' Is this claim credible?"
**Domain**: Business & Commerce / Technology

**Failure Conditions**:

## TEST 14 — Nearly Identical Subjects

**Test ID**: TC-14
**Name**: Two Similar SaaS Tools
**Input**: "Compare two project time-tracking SaaS tools: both charge $12/user/month, both have mobile apps, both integrate with Slack and Jira, both have reporting dashboards. What's actually different?"
**Domain**: Technology / Business
**Mode Activated**: Head-to-Head + Hidden Difference

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Acknowledge explicitly: at the stated feature level, these tools appear substantially equivalent
- Apply Layer 2 (Failure Mode Analysis): how does each fail when stressed?
- Apply Layer 1 (Philosophy Investigation): what does each company believe about how time tracking should work?
- Apply Layer 3 (Edge Case): what happens for edge-case users (freelancers, agencies, remote teams in different timezones)?
- Apply Layer 5 (Community): what do existing users say about why they switched from one to the other?
- Honest verdict if differences remain minimal: say so explicitly

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Acknowledgment that surface-level parity is real
- Investigation of non-obvious differences (philosophy, failure modes, support, community)
- Honest verdict: "These tools are largely equivalent on measured dimensions. The meaningful differences are [X]" or "No meaningful differentiation has been found within available evidence"
- Recommendation for what additional information would distinguish them

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not manufacture differences to satisfy the user's expectation of a differentiating analysis
- Applies deeper investigation before concluding equivalence
- Provides honest verdict even if the answer is "they're basically the same"

**Failure Conditions**:
- Finds superficial differences and declares them meaningful
- Produces a comparison that implies meaningful differentiation where none exists
- Does not apply hidden difference investigation before concluding equivalence

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Near-identical subjects; anti-manufactured-uniqueness; honest equivalence verdict

## TEST 16 — Scientific Concept Comparison

**Test ID**: TC-16
**Name**: Physics Concepts Without Conflation
**Input**: "Compare quantum entanglement and quantum tunneling as communication concepts — not for physics class, but to explain them to a business audience thinking about quantum computing"

- Establish the specific comparison context: communication to a non-technical business audience

- Explicit warning about common business-audience misconceptions

- Does not oversimplify to the point of inaccuracy
- Does not conflate scientific significance with practical relevance

**Failure Conditions**:
- Perpetuates the "quantum entanglement enables instant communication" misconception
- Declares one "more unique" without defining unique in context
- Produces a physics lecture instead of a business-audience explanation

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Scientific accuracy; audience-appropriate communication; not conflating novelty with importance

## TEST 17 — Minimal Input Analysis

**Test ID**: TC-17
**Name**: Informal Minimal Request
**Input**: "compare my bakery to starbucks"
**Domain**: Business & Commerce / Experiences
**Mode Activated**: Head-to-Head (asymmetric)

**Expected Analytical Behavior**:
- Recognize: this is an asymmetric, cross-scale comparison
- Do NOT refuse the analysis due to lack of information
- Declare: "I don't have specific information about your bakery; I'll analyze this based on what you've shared and general category characteristics. Tell me more about your bakery to make this more specific."
- Proceed: compare the category characteristics of an independent local bakery vs. Starbucks (a global QSR chain with a coffee-forward identity)
- Apply Contextual Repositioning: where an independent bakery's perceived weaknesses (smaller menu, inconsistent quality, no mobile ordering) are reframed as potential strengths (authentic, community-rooted, craft-focused)
- Apply Attribute Reversal: Starbucks' consistency is a strength for many; for some audiences it is exactly the wrong experience

**Expected Output Must Contain**:
- Acknowledgment of information gaps and invitation to provide more context
- General category comparison that is immediately useful
- Specific angles where a local bakery can and cannot compete with Starbucks
- The most important differentiation opportunity for a local bakery vs. a global chain

**Quality Pass Criteria**:
- Does not refuse to analyze due to ambiguity
- Declares assumptions clearly
- Provides immediately useful analysis even from minimal input
- Invites more information without blocking analysis on receiving it

**Failure Conditions**:
- Refuses to analyze without more information
- Treats the two subjects as direct competitors on all dimensions
- Does not identify the fundamental asymmetry (local independent vs. global chain) as itself a differentiation opportunity

**Edge Conditions Tested**: Minimal input handling; informal request; asymmetric comparison; request completeness under ambiguity
