# HEAD-TO-HEAD Workflow
## UNIQUENESS — Direct Comparison of Two Subjects

---

## ACTIVATION TRIGGERS

This workflow activates when:
- User explicitly compares exactly two named subjects ("A vs B", "compare X and Y")
- User asks what differentiates one thing from another specific thing
- User wants to choose between two alternatives
- User asks "what's unique about X compared to Y?"
- User provides two subjects without further context — assume Head-to-Head

**Do NOT activate** if the user mentions three or more subjects (use Multi-Competitor workflow) or if only one subject is named (use Uniqueness Audit workflow).

---

## PRE-ANALYSIS SETUP

Before beginning analysis, establish:

### 1. Subject Declaration
```
Subject A: [Name / Description]
Subject B: [Name / Description]
```

### 2. Relationship Type
Determine how these subjects relate:
- **Direct competitors** — same category, same audience, same purpose
- **Adjacent competitors** — different category, overlapping use case
- **Cross-category comparison** — fundamentally different categories (see Edge Cases)
- **Aspirational comparison** — user comparing their subject to a benchmark

### 3. Comparison Objective
What does the user actually want to know?
- Which is better for a specific use case?
- What is genuinely different between them?
- What hidden differences exist?
- How do they position relative to each other?

### 4. Scope Definition
- **Market scope**: Global, regional, niche
- **Time scope**: Current state, historical trajectory
- **Audience scope**: Who is the relevant evaluator?
- **Information availability**: What evidence is accessible?

---

## PHASE-BY-PHASE INSTRUCTIONS

### Phase 1 — Context Intelligence
- Identify the domain each subject belongs to
- Determine the purpose and function of each
- Establish the relevant audience and use context
- Identify the competitive universe each subject operates within

### Phase 2 — Subject Profiling
Build a concise profile for each subject:
- Core identity (what it is, what it does)
- Key characteristics
- Stated positioning and self-description
- Audience and market
- Known strengths and known limitations

### Phase 3 — Dimension Discovery
Generate the most relevant comparison dimensions for this specific pair. Do NOT use a generic template. Ask:
- What dimensions matter most to the relevant audience?
- What dimensions distinguish this category from others?
- What unusual dimensions might reveal hidden differences?
- Which dimensions can actually be evaluated with available evidence?

Minimum 6 dimensions. Maximum based on what adds value.

### Phase 4 — Similarity Mapping
Document shared characteristics:
- Functional similarities
- Positioning similarities
- Audience similarities
- Operational similarities

Classify each similarity as **Foundational** (expected in this category), **Incidental** (happens to be true), or **Strategic** (deliberately aligned).

### Phase 5 — Surface Difference Identification
Document the most visible and commonly cited differences. These are the differences most people already notice. Document them, but do not stop here — this is the floor, not the ceiling of the analysis.

### Phase 6 — Hidden Difference Excavation
Apply the Hidden Uniqueness Detector. Investigate:
- Design philosophy differences (not just design choices)
- Process and operation differences
- Value system differences
- Community and relationship differences
- Edge case behavior differences
- Failure mode differences (how each fails, and what that reveals)
- Communication style differences
- Combinational differences (ordinary features in unusual configurations)

### Phase 7 — Novelty Evaluation
For the most significant differences identified:
- How common is this characteristic within the broader category?
- Scale: Common → Less Common → Uncommon → Rare → Highly Distinctive → Potentially Unprecedented
- Assign with evidence, not assumption

### Phase 8 — Value Assessment
Not every difference matters. For each meaningful difference, evaluate:
- Does it solve a real problem better?
- Does it improve the experience?
- Does it reduce cost, friction, or risk?
- Does it create emotional or cultural value?
- Does it produce a defensible competitive advantage?
- For whom specifically does this difference matter?

### Phase 9 — Combination Analysis
Look at characteristic combinations:
- Does Subject A have an unusual combination of characteristics not found in Subject B?
- Does Subject B have an unusual combination not found in Subject A?
- What would happen if the best combination of both were realized?

### Phase 10 — Opportunity Identification
Based on what neither subject does well:
- What dimension is underserved by both?
- What audience is poorly served by both?
- What experience gap exists between them?

### Phase 11 — Strategic Recommendation
Translate findings into actionable guidance:
- What should Subject A emphasize to differentiate?
- What should Subject B emphasize?
- What would make each more distinctive?
- What is the single most defensible uniqueness for each?

### Phase 12 — Synthesis
Produce the final verdict:
- What is the most meaningful single difference?
- Who does each serve better, and why specifically?
- What is each subject's core uniqueness claim?

## DECISION TREES

### When Information is Sparse
```
Is the subject public? 
  YES → Proceed with available public evidence; classify all inferences as [INFERENCE]
  NO → Declare information limitations; analyze from what user has provided; request additional input if critical gaps exist
```

```
Is information contradictory?
  → Document the contradiction explicitly
  → Present both interpretations
  → Identify what additional evidence would resolve the contradiction
  → Do not arbitrarily select one interpretation
```

### When One Subject is Much More Well-Known
- Do not allow the well-known subject to dominate the analysis
- Apply equal investigative depth to both
- Explicitly compensate for information asymmetry by noting: "Less public information is available for Subject B; this analysis relies on [source types] and may underrepresent Subject B's characteristics"
- Avoid framing the lesser-known subject as simply "smaller" or "less advanced" — investigate what it does that the well-known subject does not

---

## EDGE CASES

### Cross-Category Comparison
When the two subjects do not belong to the same category (e.g., comparing a methodology to a software tool):
1. Declare the comparison type: "These subjects operate in different categories. The comparison will focus on [shared purpose / overlapping use case / conceptual similarity]."
2. Identify the shared comparison axis (what makes this comparison meaningful at all)
3. Only compare on dimensions where comparison is valid
4. Do not force invalid comparisons

### Intentionally Unfair Comparisons
When a user compares a niche product to a category leader (David vs. Goliath):
1. Acknowledge the asymmetry
2. Reframe: What can the smaller subject do that the category leader cannot or does not?
3. Focus on differentiation, not competition on shared dimensions where the larger subject will always win

## QUALITY CHECKS BEFORE DELIVERY

- [ ] Did I look beyond the obvious differences?
- [ ] Is every major claim supported by a stated evidence type?
- [ ] Did I identify at least one non-obvious difference?
- [ ] Did I evaluate whether differences actually create value?
- [ ] Is my final verdict specific, not generic?
- [ ] Did I avoid producing generic conclusions ("both have their strengths")?
- [ ] Did I consider combination-level uniqueness?
- [ ] Did I clearly state information limitations?

## EXAMPLE ANALYSIS BRIEF (Introduction Frame)

> **Comparing Claude and GPT-4 — Head-to-Head Intelligence Analysis**
>
> These are two of the most capable large language models available to consumers and developers. Both are frontier AI assistants trained on large corpora and capable of reasoning, writing, coding, and analysis. The comparison objective is to discover what makes each genuinely distinctive — not in terms of benchmark scores, but in terms of meaningful behavioral, philosophical, and experiential differences that matter to users.
>
> Comparison scope: Current public versions; general-purpose use; developer and professional audience; global market.
>
> Information basis: Public documentation, observed behavior, stated positioning, and community analysis. All inferences are marked [INFERENCE].
