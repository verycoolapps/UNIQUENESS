# MARKET WHITE SPACE Workflow
## UNIQUENESS — Identifying Unoccupied or Underserved Opportunities

---

## ACTIVATION TRIGGERS

This workflow activates when:
- User asks "what isn't anyone doing yet in [market]?"
- User asks "where's the gap in this market?"
- User asks "what opportunity exists that competitors are missing?"
- User wants to position a new subject and needs to find unclaimed territory
- After Multi-Competitor analysis reveals sparse zones in the position map

---

## STEP 1 — DEFINE THE COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

Before identifying what's missing, map what exists.

### 1A — Establish the Category Boundary
Define the market or category being analyzed:
- **Category name and scope**: What is this market about?
- **Inclusion criteria**: What must a subject do or be to count as a competitor in this space?
- **Exclusion criteria**: What adjacent subjects are explicitly outside this analysis?
- **Geographic scope**: Global, regional, or local?
- **Temporal scope**: Current state, or including historical players?

### 1B — Enumerate All Meaningful Players
List every significant alternative. For each, record:
- Name
- Primary positioning statement (how they describe themselves)
- Core audience
- Core value proposition
- Price tier

---

## STEP 2 — MAP EXISTING POSITIONS

### 2A — Dimension Selection for Mapping
Choose 2–3 dimensions that maximally spread the competitors across the map. Good dimensions:
- Create a wide distribution (not all subjects clustered at one end)
- Are meaningful to the target audience (not arbitrary)
- Are actionable (a subject can plausibly move along these dimensions)

Poor dimension choices:
- Dimensions where all competitors score similarly (no spread)
- Dimensions that are purely subjective without evidence ("quality")
- Dimensions that are impossible to change (founding year, country of origin)

### 2B — Position Each Competitor
For each competitor, determine its position on the chosen dimensions based on evidence. Use a consistent scale (1–5 or Low/Medium/High) for each dimension.

### 2C — Build the Position Map

```
         HIGH [Dimension Y]
         │
         │    ● C         ● F
         │
         │          ● A
         │
         │    ● E               ● B
         │          ● D
         │
         └────────────────────────────
                              HIGH [Dimension X]
```

Annotate:
- **Clusters** (groups of closely positioned competitors)
- **Sparse zones** (areas with few or no competitors)
- **Extreme positions** (subjects at the edges — often the most differentiated)

---

## STEP 3 — IDENTIFY UNDERSERVED OR UNSERVED NEEDS

### 3A — Audience Needs Mapping
Beyond where competitors sit, identify what the target audience actually needs:
- Survey the most common complaints about existing alternatives
- Identify jobs-to-be-done that no current alternative fully satisfies
- Look for workarounds — things users do to compensate for gaps in existing solutions
- Find "if only" statements — expressed desires that no current alternative meets

### 3B — Needs Not Served by Any Player
Document needs that exist in the audience but are addressed poorly or not at all by any current alternative:

```
| Unmet Need          | Currently addressed? | Best current solution | Gap severity |
|---------------------|----------------------|-----------------------|--------------|
| [Need 1]            | Partially (B, C)     | B — but limited       | High         |
| [Need 2]            | No                   | None                  | Medium       |
| [Need 3]            | Yes (A)              | A — adequate          | Low          |
```

---

## STEP 4 — EVALUATE WHITE SPACE ATTRACTIVENESS

**Critical step.** Not every empty space on a position map is a real opportunity. Evaluate each identified white space:

### The VADER Test for White Space

**V — Viable**: Is there a real business or value proposition that could occupy this space?
**A — Attractive**: Is there sufficient demand from a real audience?
**D — Defensible**: Could a subject entering this space maintain it without being immediately copied?
**E — Empty by choice or by oversight**: Did competitors avoid this space intentionally, or did they simply not think of it?
**R — Reachable**: Can a realistic actor actually access this white space given available resources, capabilities, and constraints?

| White Space     | Viable? | Attractive? | Defensible? | Empty by oversight? | Reachable? | Rating   |
|-----------------|---------|-------------|-------------|---------------------|------------|----------|
| [Space A]       | Yes     | Yes         | Medium      | Yes                 | Yes        | Strong   |
| [Space B]       | Yes     | Low         | High        | No — by choice      | Yes        | Avoid    |
| [Space C]       | Yes     | High        | Low         | Yes                 | Medium     | Moderate |

### Why Spaces Are Empty — Classification
- **Demand vacuum**: No real audience wants this combination
- **Economic barrier**: Technically possible but not viable economically
- **Regulatory barrier**: Legally restricted or compliance-heavy
- **Capability barrier**: Requires rare capabilities — potential moat if you have them
- **Cultural barrier**: Market not ready; timing issue, not opportunity absence
- **Genuine blindspot**: No one has thought of it — highest-potential category

---

## STEP 5 — RISK ASSESSMENT FOR WHITE SPACE ENTRY

For each attractive white space, evaluate risks:

### Market Risk
- Is there sufficient demand?
- Is the audience ready for this positioning?
- What evidence supports demand?

### Competitive Risk
- How quickly could incumbents respond by moving into this space?
- Does any competitor have stated plans to move here?
- What barriers protect this space once occupied?

### Execution Risk
- What capabilities are required to occupy this space?
- Does the subject entering this space have them?
- What would need to be built, hired, or acquired?

### Timing Risk
- Is this space empty now but about to be occupied?
- Or is it early — before the audience is ready?

---

## STEP 6 — PROTOTYPE A POSITIONING CONCEPT

For the highest-potential white space identified, draft a positioning concept:

### Positioning Concept Template
```
POSITION NAME: [Short, memorable label for this space]

POSITIONING STATEMENT:
"For [target audience] who [specific unmet need],
[Subject] is the [category] that [core differentiator]
unlike [alternatives] which [limitation]."

CORE CHARACTERISTICS REQUIRED:
1. [What the subject must do to credibly occupy this space]
2. ...

AUDIENCE FIT:
[Who specifically would respond to this positioning — with evidence]

DIFFERENTIATION CLAIM:
[What would make this position distinctive and defensible]

WHITE SPACE VALIDATION SCORE:
VADER: V[score] A[score] D[score] E[score] R[score]
Overall: [Strong / Moderate / Speculative]
```
