# UNIQUENESS AUDIT Workflow
## UNIQUENESS — Complete Audit of a Single Subject

---

## ACTIVATION TRIGGERS

This workflow activates when:
- User provides one subject and asks "what makes this unique?"
- User asks "audit my [business/product/brand/idea]"
- User asks "is this actually unique?"
- User provides one subject without naming a comparator
- User asks for a uniqueness score or rating for one subject

This is the deepest, most comprehensive workflow. It treats one subject as the subject of investigation, drawing on a defined alternative universe for benchmarking — not direct head-to-head comparison.

---

## AUDIT PROTOCOL OVERVIEW

A full Uniqueness Audit consists of six sequential investigative layers:

1. **Internal Uniqueness Assessment** — What is it actually?
2. **External Uniqueness Assessment** — How does it compare to alternatives?
3. **Audience Perception Audit** — How is it perceived?
4. **Communication Audit** — Is its uniqueness communicated effectively?
5. **Opportunity Gap Audit** — What uniqueness could exist but doesn't yet?
6. **Scoring and Verdict** — Structured evaluation and final conclusions

---

## LAYER 1 — INTERNAL UNIQUENESS ASSESSMENT

**Objective:** Understand the subject completely on its own terms before comparing it to anything.

### 1A — Identity Investigation
- What is the subject's stated purpose?
- What is its actual purpose (which may differ from stated)?
- What is its core mechanism — how does it do what it does?
- What are its foundational design decisions?
- What constraints or values shaped it?

### 1B — Characteristic Inventory
List all notable characteristics across relevant dimensions:
- Functional characteristics (what it does)
- Design characteristics (how it looks/feels)
- Operational characteristics (how it works internally)
- Economic characteristics (how it creates/captures value)
- Relational characteristics (how it relates to its users/audience)
- Cultural characteristics (what values, beliefs, or worldview it embodies)

### 1C — Origin Analysis
- Where did this come from?
- What problem or insight prompted its creation?
- Has it evolved from something else?
- What decisions shaped it into its current form?

### 1D — Self-Differentiation Claims
- What does the subject claim makes it unique (marketing, positioning, founder statements)?
- Are these claims specific or generic?
- Are they verifiable?
- Classify each claim: **Verified** / **Plausible** / **Unverified** / **Contradicted by evidence**

---

## LAYER 2 — EXTERNAL UNIQUENESS ASSESSMENT

**Objective:** Measure the subject against relevant alternatives to establish where genuine differentiation exists.

### 2A — Define the Comparison Universe
Who are the most relevant alternatives? Define:
- **Direct alternatives** (same function, same audience)
- **Adjacent alternatives** (similar function, different audience — or different function, same audience)
- **Aspirational alternatives** (what the subject aspires to be)
- **Historical alternatives** (predecessors or early-stage versions of this concept)

### 2B — Dimension-Level Comparison
For each key characteristic identified in Layer 1, assess:
- How common is this characteristic across the comparison universe?
- Scoring: **Standard in category / Less common / Uncommon / Rare / Highly distinctive / Potentially unprecedented**
- Evidence for classification

### 2C — Combination Analysis
Even if individual characteristics are common, the **combination** may be distinctive:
- Identify the most unusual combination of characteristics
- Assess how often this combination appears together across the comparison universe
- A subject composed of individually ordinary elements can be genuinely distinctive through unique combination

### 2D — External Differentiation Map
```
| Characteristic    | Subject | Category Norm | Distinctiveness   |
|-------------------|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| [Feature A]       | Yes     | Yes (80%)     | Standard          |
| [Feature B]       | Yes     | No (15%)      | Uncommon          |
| [Feature C]       | Unique  | No (3%)       | Rare              |
| [A + B + C combo] | Yes     | No (1%)       | Highly Distinctive|
```

## LAYER 3 — AUDIENCE PERCEPTION AUDIT

**Objective:** Understand how the subject's target audience (and actual audience) perceives its uniqueness — which may differ from its actual uniqueness.

### 3A — Perception Sources
- Customer/user reviews and testimonials
- Community discussions
- Press and media coverage
- Social signals
- Word-of-mouth descriptors ("they're the ones who...")

### 3B — Perception-Reality Gap
Compare what the audience perceives as unique vs. what analysis reveals as actually unique:

| Perceived as Unique         | Actually Unique?            | Gap Analysis                     |
|-----------------------------|-----------------------------|----------------------------------|
| "Their customer service"    | Verifiably above-average    | Perception matches reality       |
| "Their design"              | Similar to competitors      | Perception exceeds reality — risk|
| "Their technology"          | Genuinely rare mechanism    | Underperceived — opportunity     |

### 3C — Perception Risks
- **Overperceived uniqueness**: Audiences believe it's more unique than it is — fragile, can collapse when reality is revealed
- **Underperceived uniqueness**: Genuine differentiation that audiences don't notice — wasted asset, communication opportunity

---

## LAYER 4 — COMMUNICATION AUDIT

**Objective:** Is the subject's actual uniqueness being communicated clearly and compellingly?

### 4A — Uniqueness Communication Review
Examine: website, tagline, marketing materials, product description, stated value proposition.

For each genuine uniqueness finding from Layers 1-3, ask:
- Is this uniqueness explicitly mentioned anywhere?
- If mentioned, is it explained in a way that makes the significance clear?
- If not mentioned, why? (Overlooked? Assumed? Too technical to communicate?)

### 4B — Communication Quality Scale
- **5 — Unmistakable**: The uniqueness is impossible to miss and immediately compelling
- **4 — Clear**: The uniqueness is stated and explained
- **3 — Present but weak**: The uniqueness is mentioned but not given appropriate emphasis
- **2 — Buried**: The uniqueness exists in fine print, deep pages, or technical documentation
- **1 — Invisible**: Genuine uniqueness is not communicated at all

### 4C — Communication Recommendations
For each uniqueness finding rated below 4, provide a specific communication recommendation.

---

## LAYER 5 — OPPORTUNITY GAP AUDIT

**Objective:** Discover uniqueness that could exist but doesn't yet — where could this subject become more distinctive?

### 5A — Dimension Gap Scan
For every dimension assessed, ask: "Is there an adjacent position on this dimension that is unoccupied and potentially valuable?"

### 5B — Combination Opportunity Scan
What unusual feature/characteristic combinations exist in adjacent categories that haven't been applied to this subject's category?

### 5C — Audience Gap Scan
Is there an underserved audience whose needs this subject could uniquely address — but currently doesn't?

### 5D — Future Uniqueness Projection
What trends, technologies, or market shifts in the next 3–5 years could make this subject more distinctive — if it positioned for them now?

## SCORING AND VERDICT

### Uniqueness Score Calculation

| Dimension              | Weight | Score (0–10) | Weighted |
|------------------------|--------|--------------|----------|
| Distinctiveness        | 20%    | X            | X×0.20   |
| Rarity                 | 10%    | X            | X×0.10   |
| Originality            | 15%    | X            | X×0.15   |
| Meaningfulness         | 15%    | X            | X×0.15   |
| Value Creation         | 15%    | X            | X×0.15   |
| Audience Relevance     | 10%    | X            | X×0.10   |
| Defensibility          | 5%     | X            | X×0.05   |
| Combination Strength   | 10%    | X            | X×0.10   |
| **TOTAL**              | 100%   |              | **X/10** |

**Always explain the score. Never present it without justification.**

### Verdict Classification
- **8.5–10.0**: Unmistakably Distinctive — strong, defensible uniqueness across multiple dimensions
- **7.0–8.4**: Strongly Distinctive — clear differentiation with some vulnerability
- **5.5–6.9**: Moderately Distinctive — meaningful differentiation in specific areas; broad similarity elsewhere
- **3.5–5.4**: Slightly Distinctive — some differentiation exists but is limited or fragile
- **0.0–3.4**: Limited Differentiation — largely indistinguishable from alternatives; significant opportunity needed
